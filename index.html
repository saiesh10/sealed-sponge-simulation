About This Model: Physics, Boundary Conditions & Assumptions

1. Isotropic Scattering (g = 0)

Photon scattering directions are chosen with uniform probability on the unit sphere (cos θ ∈ [−1, 1], φ ∈ [0, 2π]). Real submicron polymer voids exhibit forward-peaked Mie scattering (anisotropy factor g ≈ 0.7–0.85). The model uses the transport mean free path ℓt = ℓs / (1 − g) as the effective isotropic step length.

2. Zero Absorption (µa = 0)

The PMMA scaffold and pore air are treated as completely non-absorbing across the visible spectrum (λ = 400–700 nm). Real polymers and solvent binders have minor extinction at short wavelengths and near-IR overtone bands, which are neglected here to isolate purely structural scattering.

3. No Boundary Fresnel Reflection

Internal reflection at the top film-ambient boundary (nfilm ≈ 1.49 vs air = 1.0) is not modeled. In real films, total internal reflection redirects ~10–15% of forward-scattered photons back into the film, which explains why Syurik et al. (2018) measure ~75% reflectance at 4 µm while this isotropic random walk produces ~69%.

4. No Polarization

Photons are treated as scalar energy packets undergoing random Poisson walk events without tracking transverse electric and magnetic polarization vectors or Stokes parameters.

5. Geometric / Wave Limit

Thin-film wave interference, coherent backscattering cones, and speckle are omitted. The model operates within classical radiative transfer theory, valid when the transport mean free path ℓt is much larger than the optical wavelength (ℓt >> λ).

6. Independent Scattering

Void scattering centers are assumed to act independently without near-field evanescent coupling or optical crowding saturation. Real paints operate near 30–50% void volume fractions where dependent scattering effects can emerge.
